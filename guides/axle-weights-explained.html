Motorhome axle weights: front, rear, and why the total isn’t enough

Motorhome axle weights matter because the law cares about each axle, not only the total. Overload a rear axle and you can fail a check even if you’re under MAM overall. Front and rear are two different loads.

Why axles matter

A weighbridge total answers “how heavy is the van today?” It does not prove each axle is inside its own limit. If you have not been yet, start with the motorhome weighbridge how-to.

Two different numbers

What you have What it means
VIN plate axle ratings Maximum the axle is allowed to carry
Weighbridge axle weights What that axle is carrying today

Never treat the plate as today’s weight. The plate is the cap; the ticket is the reading. For the plated van maximum (MAM) versus paperwork empty, see MAM, Mass in Service and payload.

Front vs rear loading

  • Front often carries engine, cab, some kit.
  • Rear often carries habitation, tanks, garage, people at the back.
  • Filling water or loading the garage usually hits the rear hardest.

Empty vs Loaded checks

  • Empty: base van — useful for “what’s left for kit?”
  • Loaded: as driven — useful for “am I legal right now?”

What we won’t do

We will not invent front/rear kilograms from a list of people and boxes. Only a ticket (or a careful two-pad weigh) gives axle weights.
