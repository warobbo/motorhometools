Leisure battery size in plain English

Leisure battery size in plain English means decoding the big number on the case (often Ah) and why you should not plan to empty it every night.

What this is for

The label is a maker figure, not a daily “use this much” promise. First list what you actually run — see the daily power budget — then match that to your bank, not a guessed size.

Ah in one sentence

Amp-hours (Ah) is a maker label for how much charge the battery can hold under their test conditions — not a promise of how you should use it every day.

Usable capacity (do not invent a %)

Many lead-acid leisure batteries are harmed if you routinely drain them flat. Lithium chemistries have different rules. Check your battery maker’s handbook for depth-of-discharge guidance. We will not invent a “safe %” here.

If a fitter installed the bank, their notes beat a generic internet rule of thumb.

Parallel banks and age

Two batteries in parallel are not always “double” in real life if one is weak. Age and cold weather cut available power — again, handbook and measured voltage or state of charge beat guesses.

Roof solar can top the bank up, but it will not fix a budget that is already too big. See the solar reality check.

Sources
